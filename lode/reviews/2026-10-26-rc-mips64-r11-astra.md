**Verification of the r10 resolution**

The original child-death hang is resolved:

- Prepare inserts negative-ticket child records before `fork()`. Resize tracking updates their `min_size`, and child registration changes only their PID.
- The pipe path waits for EOF and removes unclaimed ticket records.
- The no-pipe parent path contains no acknowledgment wait. Failed forks drop their tickets; successful forks leave records for the child to claim under `vm_lock()`.
- No child records means no handshake pipe allocation.
- Emergency descriptors attempt allocation at `>=512`; if duplication fails, the implementation retains the original lower descriptors.

However, the new compaction path can discard a **live** child’s records.

**Detailed findings**

**Medium — Full-table compaction mistakes a delayed child’s ticket for an abandoned ticket.**

At [vm_file.c:139](/Users/angerman/Projects/zw3rk/mips64rt/src/vm/vm_file.c:139), compaction removes any negative-PID record differing from the caller’s process-local `vm_pending_ticket`. After a successful no-pipe fork, the parent clears that variable before returning. This does not establish that the child died.

A concrete source-derived failure sequence is:

1. The parent has 64 private mappings of one inode. Prepare inserts 64 child records, filling all 128 slots.
2. Pipe allocation fails even after releasing reserves. Fork succeeds, but the child remains unscheduled.
3. The parent performs a host-page-aligned `mprotect`. Its protection hook calls compaction, which deletes one of the live child’s ticket records.
4. The parent shrinks and regrows the file.
5. The child claims the remaining records. The deleted mapping is never reconstructed, so its inherited dirty pages miss shrink reconciliation and retain stale data.

Setting `in->wide = 1` does not repair this: its only consumer at [vm_file.c:533](/Users/angerman/Projects/zw3rk/mips64rt/src/vm/vm_file.c:533) broadens DBT invalidation; it does not restore mapping metadata or EOF/COW tracking.

Retain unclaimed tickets until abandonment is established, or provide a lossless overflow mechanism. This finding concerns eviction of successfully reserved records, rather than the older general capacity limit. It was independently cross-checked against the source, not runtime-reproduced.
